Massive galaxy clusters act as natural telescopes: their gravity magnifies the light of galaxies far behind them. Using *Hubble Space Telescope* observations of lensing clusters, I have searched for some of the most distant galaxies known, which formed within the first few hundred million years after the Big Bang.

In *Hubble* imaging from the CLASH survey, we found three magnified images of MACS0647-JD, a galaxy at *z*≈11 lensed by a massive foreground cluster. When it was announced, MACS0647-JD was the most distant galaxy candidate known.
