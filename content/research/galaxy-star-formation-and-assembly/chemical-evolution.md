Every generation of stars enriches the gas in its host galaxy with heavy elements, while inflows of pristine gas and outflows driven by feedback dilute or remove them. Measuring the chemical abundances of galaxies therefore records the history of their star formation and of the gas flowing in and out of them.

The most reliable way to measure a galaxy's metallicity uses faint "auroral" emission lines that reveal the temperature of its ionized gas, but those lines are faint and hard to detect. With DESI we measured direct abundances of nitrogen, oxygen, neon, sulfur, and argon for 49,959 galaxies, mapping the mass-metallicity relation over five orders of magnitude in stellar mass and finding the two most metal-poor star-forming galaxies known in the nearby universe.
