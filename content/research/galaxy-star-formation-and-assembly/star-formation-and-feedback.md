*Star formation* is the process of converting cold gas (mostly hydrogen and helium) into new stars powered by thermonuclear fusion. The most massive stars then inject radiative and mechanical *feedback* into the surrounding interstellar medium through stellar winds and, at the end of their lives, powerful supernova explosions, which can affect future episodes of star formation. Supermassive black holes can also be a source of feedback at various stages of their growth.

Through detailed observations of both nearby and distant galaxies, my work aims to calibrate how we measure star formation in galaxies, and to understand how feedback from massive stars and black holes regulates it.
