Physics and astrophysics are hard, and every student learns new material in a different way, so no single teaching method reaches everyone. I mix group problem-solving in a flipped classroom, peer instruction, inquiry-based and computational activities, and traditional lectures with slides and board work. Whatever the method, my goal is the same: to give every student frequent chances to engage with me, with their classmates, and with the material in a welcoming and inclusive classroom.

## How I Teach

<div class="approach">

- **Learning together.** I tell my students that we are taking a journey through the material together, and I want them to see me make mistakes as I work through problems at the board. Science advances through inquiry, not memorization, so students meet new concepts in their reading before class, and class time goes to synthesis and problem-solving.
- **Think-Pair-Share.** Students answer a conceptual question by voting all at once with colored ABCD cards. If fewer than about two-thirds are right (or the whole class is wrong, which happens more often than you might expect), they discuss it with a partner and vote again, while I listen in and bring common misconceptions to the whole class.
- **Group whiteboarding.** Groups of two to four students work through problems and interactive, quantitative worksheets on whiteboards while I circulate, answer questions, and give hints. Groups often share their solutions with the class, which builds confidence and lets students compare their thinking.
- **Listening to students.** Anonymous mid-semester surveys, whose results we discuss as a class, let me adjust a course while it is still under way. Between classes, I use online tools to keep students engaged with the material and with each other.

</div>
